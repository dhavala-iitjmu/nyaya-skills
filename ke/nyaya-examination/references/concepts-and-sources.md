# Nyāya: conceptual scope and sources

## Historical anchor and selective adaptation

Nyāya is a developed epistemological, metaphysical and argumentative tradition,
not a synonym for criticism or a generic debate protocol. This skill selects
knowledge-source assessment, inference examination and settlement of doubt. It
does not reproduce the sixteen topics, the full theories of cognition, self and
liberation, or the technical apparatus of Navya-Nyāya. Those omissions are scope
limits, not assertions that the historical discipline contained only these steps.

The Nyāya-sūtra and its commentaries are distinct textual layers; later treatments
develop their terminology and classifications. Explain the actual inferential
defect before attaching a term. Do not present the later familiar list of
asiddha/savyabhicāra/viruddha/satpratipakṣa/bādhita as the exact unchanged wording
of Nyāya-sūtra 1.2.4. A task requiring precise historical exegesis needs a specified
text, commentary and translation.

## Distinctions that must change the examination

- **Means versus warranted instance:** an acquisition process may appear reliable
  yet fail in this case. Labeling an item perception or testimony does not make it
  knowledge. Explain the subject's actual access and relevant defects.
- **Upamāna versus unrestricted analogy:** the familiar recognition example links
  a previously described unfamiliar kind with a perceived exemplar. Transfer of
  arbitrary properties from a similar object needs another warranted argument.
- **Pakṣadharmatā versus vyāpti:** establishing that the reason is present here is
  different from establishing its dependable connection with what is to be proved.
  A good observation of the reason can coexist with a bad inference.
- **Restriction versus exception suppression:** identify a condition under which
  the reason licenses the conclusion. In scientific tasks, distinguish statistical
  evidence and domain-limited generalization from exceptionless pervasion; this
  adaptation cannot turn a finite sample into a universal law.
- **Tarka versus new evidence:** test conditional consequences against established
  premises. A contradiction may defeat a proposed account; imagining a favorable
  consequence does not independently establish an empirical premise.
- **Nirṇaya versus consensus:** settlement answers the specified doubt with
  adequate warrant. Repeated model outputs or a dialogue's polite agreement do not
  supply missing premises. One agent's internal roles share a source/model context.

For a supplied policy rule, identify it as a stipulated premise, then check its
application. That is an inference within a convention, not proof that the rule
is a natural necessity or that satisfying it grants execution authority.

## Reading map

Primary translations below are historical English editions. Their terms may
differ from current scholarship; read the sūtra separately from its commentary.
No translation is treated as a complete or uncontroversial modern reconstruction.

1. Ganganatha Jha, *Nyāya Sūtras of Gautama with commentaries* (1915),
   [1.1.3: knowledge sources and commentary on enumeration, definition, examination](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/nyaya-sutras-of-gautama-with-commentaries/d/doc1907017.html).
   Anchor for the four sources and examining definitions rather than merely
   naming them.
2. Same edition, [1.1.6: upamāna](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/nyaya-sutras-of-gautama-with-commentaries/d/doc1907020.html)
   and [1.1.7: śabda](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/nyaya-sutras-of-gautama-with-commentaries/d/doc1907021.html).
   Consult for resemblance/recognition and trustworthy testimony; do not treat
   statistical similarity or a bare URL as sufficient warrant.
3. Same edition, [1.1.32: five members](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/book/nyaya-sutras-of-gautama-with-commentaries/d/doc1907049.html).
   A presentation for establishing a claim to another; use its logical roles,
   not a ceremonial five-line template.
4. [Internet Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Nyāya](https://iep.utm.edu/nyaya/),
   sections on inference, defeaters, suppositional reasoning, analogy and testimony.
   Scholarly secondary orientation for terminology and developments across texts;
   also makes clear that epistemology does not exhaust the historical system.

## Representation and authority

Expose enough of the argument for another participant to inspect its warrant.
This is an auditable result, not a demand for hidden chain-of-thought or a long
self-dialogue. Reusing a method, even under several roles, creates no independent
verification. Skill selection is a methodological choice within host authority;
it neither replaces that authority nor modifies the agent's model/runtime.
